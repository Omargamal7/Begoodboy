Station 1 · Lesson 4

Exponents and the order of operations

What an exponent means

An exponent is a short way to write repeated multiplication. The base is the number being multiplied. The exponent (also called the power or index) says how many times the base is used.

2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 2 to the power of 5 = 32

Expanded form → exponential form → simplest form (standard form).

Rules to remember

Any number to the power 1 is itself: 7 to the power of 1 = 7

1 to any power is 1: 1 to the power of 5 = 1 × 1 × 1 × 1 × 1 = 1

Power 2 is called squared: 7 to the power of 2 is "the square of 7".

Power 3 is called cubed: 7 to the power of 3 is "the cube of 7".

Trap: 3² is not 3 × 2

3 to the power of 2 = 3 × 3 = 9, but 3 × 2 = 6.

2 to the power of 3 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, but 2 × 3 = 6.

The exponent counts the copies of the base. It is never a number you multiply by.

The order of operations ladder

Always climb down the ladder. Never skip a rung.

1Brackets: ( ) first, then [ ]. Work from the innermost brackets out.
2Exponents: work out every power.
3Multiply and divide, from left to right.
4Add and subtract, from left to right.

Two things students forget

× and ÷ are on the same rung. Do whichever comes first from the left. In 24 ÷ 6 × 2, divide first: 4 × 2 = 8.

+ and − are on the same rung too. In 13 − 7 + 8, subtract first: 6 + 8 = 14.

A number touching a bracket means multiply: 6(3) means 6 × 3.

    Evaluating algebraic expressions

    Variables are boxes for numbers

    A variable is a letter that stands for a number, such as k, x or h. An algebraic expression mixes numbers, variables and operations, such as 7k − 15.

    7k means 7 × k. The multiplication sign is hidden.

    Two moves to evaluate

    1. Substitute. Replace the letter with its number. Write the × back in.

    2. Simplify with the order of operations ladder.

    7k − 15 at k = 6 → 7 × 6 − 15 = 42 − 15 = 27

    Careful with the letter next to a number

    If k = 6, then 7k is 7 × 6 = 42, never 76.

    If h = 3, then 5h to the power of 2 is 5 × 3², so square first: 5 × 9 = 45.

    Decimals work the same way: 8x at x = 0.5 is 8 × 0.5 = 4.

      Word problems: from a story to an expression

      Find the thing that changes

      The amount that can change (how many T-shirts, how many km, how many days) becomes the variable.

      The amount that is the same every time is a number.

      "Each" or "per" means multiply

      100 L.E. each for x T-shirts → 100x

      A coupon off the whole bill is taken away once: 100x − 40

      A starting amount or base fare is added once: 15 + 8k

      Then evaluate

      Cost of 4 T-shirts: put x = 4.

      100 × 4 − 40 = 400 − 40 = 360 L.E.

      Explain what each symbol means: x = number of T-shirts, 100 = price of one, 40 = coupon.

      Station 4 · Lesson 7

      Equivalent algebraic expressions

      What "equivalent" means

      Two expressions are equivalent if they give the same value for every value of the variable.

      2x + 10 and 2(x + 1) + 8: at x = 3 both give 16, at x = 5 both give 20. Equivalent.

      One difference is enough

      If you try a value and the answers are different, stop. They are not equivalent. No need to try another value.

      Watch out: 3x + 1 and 2x + 5 are equal at x = 4 (both 13), but different at x = 3. One equal answer does not prove anything.

      The bracket shortcut

      A number outside a bracket multiplies everything inside:

      3(x + 2) = 3x + 6

      A common mistake is 2(x + 3) = 2x + 3. The 3 must be doubled too: 2x + 6.

      Station 5 · Unit 4 Lesson 1

      Solving algebraic equations

      Expression or equation?

      An expression has no equals sign: x + 5.

      An equation says two sides are equal: x + 5 = 9.

      Solving means finding the value of the unknown that makes it true. Here x = 4.

      Way 1: mental math

      16 + x = 20

      Ask: "What number plus 16 equals 20?" The answer is 4, so x = 4.

      Way 2: inverse operations

      Undo what is being done to the letter:

      + is undone by −, − by +, × by ÷, ÷ by ×.

      y − 3 = 8 → y = 8 + 3 = 11

      4n = 28 → n = 28 ÷ 4 = 7

      Always check

      Put your answer back in. If y = 11: 11 − 3 = 8 ✓.

      If both sides are equal, your answer is right.

      Lab

      The balance

      An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other side and it stays level. The orange bag is the unknown.
