

# **Grade 6 ICT Interactive Sheets Plan**

Oct 4, 2026 · @Omar Grade 5

Twelve interactive sheets, one per lesson of the Primary 6 first-term ICT booklet, all built on the same predictable layout: a visual schedule strip, a First-Then board, three picture facts, one hands-on activity, then the booklet's own questions shown one at a time. Every design rule below is taken from a named source (National Autistic Society, UK Home Office autism design poster, NCAEP 2020 evidence-based practices, the Autism Speaks visual supports toolkit, CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0), and every lesson fact is taken from the uploaded booklet only.

The rules fall into three groups: how the sheet looks, how it speaks, and how the work is structured. Each rule is applied the same way on all 12 sheets.

| Same layout on every sheet: schedule strip top, First-Then box, facts, activity, check, done. | Soft colours, one accent colour, text contrast at least 4.5:1, no bright clashing colours, no animations or sound effects | Poster: "Use simple colours… Don't use bright contrasting colours… Use a colour contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1" |

## **The one sheet layout used for all 12 lessons**

Each sheet is six parts in a fixed order, and Grade 6 ticks each part off on the strip at the top. A sheet is meant for one sitting; if a sitting is shorter than the sheet, the strip shows where to stop and where to restart next time.

1. **Schedule strip** (top of page or screen): six small pictures in a row, one per part below, each with a tick box. A "surprise" card sits in the card set, unused unless the day changes.  
2. **First-Then box**: "First: \[this sheet's picture\]  Then: \[Grade 6’s reward picture\]". The reward is given as soon as part 6 is ticked.  
3. **Three fact cards**: the lesson's three most-tested facts, one per card, each as picture \+ one sentence in plain words \+ the exact booklet term in bold. The adult reads the card, waits 10 seconds, and asks Grade 6 to point to the picture that matches the term.  
4. **One hands-on activity**: a sort, a build, a match or a real step on the device. Steps are numbered, one action per step, one picture per step. Where the booklet allows it, the content is Grade 6’s special interest.  
5. **Check**: the booklet's own questions for that lesson, one per card. True/false items appear as a tick card and a cross card to point to. Multiple-choice items appear as three option cards, and the question line shows the blank filled only after the choice. The fact card is shown again after a wrong answer, then the question is repeated with the same words.  
6. **Done**: Grade 6 ticks the last box, the adult names what he did ("You matched all six devices"), and the Then reward follows immediately.

Two fixed rules protect the check from the booklet's trickiest format. Many booklet true/false statements are deliberately false (for example "Wireless networks use physical wiring and cables"). The NAS notes negatives are easy to mishear, so the correct fact is always taught on a fact card before its false twin appears in the check. Second, a sheet's check never introduces a term that was not on a fact card or in the activity.

## **How to build and run the sheets**

Build one master sheet first, test it on Theme 1 Lesson 1, and only then copy it for the other eleven. The toolkit's own advice is to introduce a First-Then board and a schedule on a task the child already does willingly, so the first sheet should be the one closest to Grade 6’s interest, not necessarily Lesson 1\.

1. **Fill the blanks in the first section.** Interest, reward, answer mode, reading level and device decide the pictures, the card count and whether the digital or paper version is built first.  
2. **Build the master in two forms.** Paper: A4 landscape, cards cut from card stock, Velcro or tray for sorts. Digital: one page per card, one "Show next" button, no timer sounds, no animation, colours checked at 4.5:1. The same pictures are used in both so nothing new has to be learned when switching.  
3. **Teach the strip and the First-Then box once, on a short easy sheet,** before any content. The toolkit says the Then reward must follow every time or the board stops being trusted.  
4. **Run one sheet per sitting.** Adult reads a card, waits 10 seconds, repeats the same words once if needed, and lets Grade 6 answer in his chosen way. Hands-on steps are done by Grade 6; the adult points to the next picture rather than speaking (prompting, then time delay, per NCAEP).  
5. **Keep the booklet in the loop.** After the check, the matching booklet page is filled in with the answers Grade 6 just gave, so the binder the school expects is complete without a second round of questions.  
6. **Record two numbers per sheet** on the back: cards answered right first time, and minutes to Done. A sheet that takes longer than the sitting length from section 1 is split at a strip boundary next time.  
7. **Change one thing at a time.** If a sheet fails, change either the pictures, the card count or the reward, never all three, so the cause is known.
