## Source defects and ambiguous items

Every issue below is retained in the ledger. Items with no defensible unique answer are manual and unscored.
- Printed p5, CW Activity 2 Q1 (u1-l1-vocab-q1): Both buy and sell are grammatical in the isolated sentence; no context rules out sell.
- Printed p6, CW Activity 1 Q5 (u1-l2-grammar-q5): The sentence permits more than one frequency adverb; the source does not provide a unique choice.
- Printed p17, Weekly Assessment B Q5 (u2-weekly-b-q5): Both still and always are grammatical choices; the isolated sentence does not give a unique answer.
- Printed p21, CW Activity 1 A Q4 (u3-l1-reading-q2): Defective printed choices: them refers to children, but choices are men/women/women/women. Do not assign a correct choice.
- Printed p23, CW Activity 2 Q3 (u3-l2-form-q3): Can they...? and Can’t they...? are both grammatical and can receive Yes, they can. No unique correction is established.
- Printed p30, Review Activity 1 Q5 (u3-review-choice-q5): Printed typo: “your” should be “you”. Show the corrected model and leave this malformed item unscored.
- Printed p30, Review Activity 2 B Q4 (u3-review-reading-q4): Source typo: “Why do you tourists” appears to mean “Why do tourists”.

Additional source quirks:
- Unit 1 review continues on printed p12 under an inconsistent Unit 2/My Family and I header; the review is assigned to Unit 1 based on sequence and its Review on Unit (1) heading.
- Unit 2 Lesson 3 study word-bank paragraph has five blanks but only four numbered labels. All five blanks are included.
- Unit 2 weekly C has four questions, not five. Nothing was invented to pad it.
- Unit 3 Lesson 2 ordering has printed question numbers 1, 2, 4. All three printed tasks are included.
- Unit 3 Lesson 3, The Proud Rose, actually has sandwich and ice cream reading passages. They are included literally rather than replacing them with a rose story.
- Unit 2 Lessons 4–5 heading Wadi El Rayan has a community-volunteering passage; Unit 3 Lessons 4–5 hero-biography heading has nutrition and Sahara passages. The actual passages determine the questions.
- The sandwich date 1750 and the Nile longest-river statement are answered as details within the supplied passages, without representing separate fact-checking.

## Answer method

Closed grammar answers are worked English-language-rule answers. Factual comprehension uses the printed passage. Open comprehension and every writing prompt require parent review and are never scored with brittle keyword matching. Natural American spelling is valid; model passages preserve the source’s mixed American/British spellings. No official answer key was fabricated.
