Fix-it: three things to get right

These come from the pages in your binder. Practise them first.

1. "All factors" means all of them

2 × 3 × 3 = 18. Its factors are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.

Do not forget the small ones: the primes 2 and 3 are factors too. Use a T-chart: 1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6.

2. GCF and LCM from prime factors

8 = 2 × 2 × 2
10 = 2 × 5

GCF: only the shared primes → 2.

LCM: shared primes once + all the leftovers → 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 40.

3. Check the size of your answer

GCF can't be bigger than the smaller number. For 15 cm and 25 cm strips, 25 is too big: the answer is 5 cm.

LCM can't be smaller than the bigger number.

Station 1 · Unit 2 Lesson 4

Prime numbers and factor trees

Factors

A factor divides a number exactly, with nothing left over. Factors come in pairs that multiply to the number.

12 = 1 × 12 = 2 × 6 = 3 × 4

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

Prime or composite?

7 A prime number has exactly 2 factors: 1 and itself.

12 A composite number has more than 2 factors.

1 The number 1 is neither. It has only one factor.

2 is the only even prime. The smallest odd prime is 3.

Three ways to find all the factors

T-chart: write pairs down the chart. 18: 1 | 18, 2 | 9, 3 | 6. Stop when the pairs meet.

Factor rainbow: write 1 2 3 6 9 18 in a line and join each pair with an arc: 1–18, 2–9, 3–6.

Factor tree: best for prime factors (see below).

Quick divisibility tricks

2 is a factor of every even number (ones digit 0, 2, 4, 6, 8).

3 is a factor when the digits add up to a multiple of 3: 27 → 2 + 7 = 9 ✓.

5 is a factor when the ones digit is 0 or 5.

Factor trees

Split a composite number into any factor pair. Keep splitting the composite branches until every branch ends in a prime. Circle the primes.

Multiply the circled primes back together: this is the prime factorization.

24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3

Any tree you draw for 24 ends with the same primes.

Station 2 · Unit 2 Lesson 5

Greatest common factor (GCF)

Method 1: list the factors

List the factors of both numbers. Circle the ones they share: these are the common factors. The biggest one is the GCF.

20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28

Common: 1, 2, 4. GCF = 4

Method 2: prime factors

Write both prime factorizations. Multiply the primes they share.

12 = 2 × 2 × 3
18 = 2 × 3 × 3

Both have one 2 and one 3, so GCF = 2 × 3 = 6

When do we need the GCF?

When we split things into equal groups and want the groups as big as possible, or as many identical boxes as possible, with nothing left over.

30 pens and 45 notebooks: GCF = 15, so 15 identical gift boxes, each with 2 pens and 3 notebooks.

Station 3 · Unit 2 Lessons 6–7

Multiples and least common multiple (LCM)

Multiples

The multiples of a number are what you get when you skip count by it: multiply it by 1, 2, 3, 4…

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30…

A number has a limited number of factors but unlimited multiples.

Common multiples and LCM

Multiples that both numbers share are common multiples. The smallest one (not 0) is the LCM.

4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24
6: 6, 12, 18, 24

Common: 12, 24… LCM = 12

When do we need the LCM?

When things repeat and we want to know when they happen together again, or the smallest amount to buy so two pack sizes match.

Hend laps every 6 minutes, Jana every 8. They meet at the start after LCM(6, 8) = 24 minutes.

Your classwork's method: stack the primes

8  = 2 × 2 × 2
12 = 2 × 2    × 3

Write the prime factors so matching primes sit on top of each other.

GCF = the matching pairs only: 2 × 2 = 4.

LCM = each matching pair once, plus every prime with no partner: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 24.

Facts from your binder

0 is a common multiple of all numbers (we start counting from the first one after 0).

Every number is a multiple of 1 and of itself.

The product of two numbers is always a common multiple: 35 = 5 × 7, so 35 is a common multiple of 5 and 7.

Station 4 · Unit 2 Lesson 8

Factors or multiples? GCF or LCM?

Read for the clue words

GCF
split, share, divide into equal groups, as wide/large as possible, greatest number of identical bags, nothing left over
LCM
again, together, at the same time, every … days, smallest number to buy, same number of each

A quick check

The GCF is never bigger than the smaller number.

The LCM is never smaller than the bigger number.

If your answer breaks this rule, you used the wrong one.

Factors or multiples?

Arranging 12 balls or 48 seats in equal rows → factors (you are splitting).

Joining 8 m ribbons: 8, 16, 24, 32… → multiples (you are repeating).

Station 5 · Unit 3 Lessons 1–2

The area model and the distributive property

Powers of ten first

Multiplying by 10, 100 or 1,000 adds 1, 2 or 3 zeros.

5 × 1,000 = 5,000
40 × 200 = 8,000

For 40 × 200: do 4 × 2 = 8, then add the 3 zeros.

The area model

Break each number into place value parts (expanded form). Draw a rectangle with one row or column per part. Multiply each little box, then add all the boxes.

234 × 27 → 200 + 30 + 4 by 20 + 7

4,000 + 600 + 80 + 1,400 + 210 + 28 = 6,318

The distributive property

8 × 9 is the same as 8 × (5 + 4) = 8 × 5 + 8 × 4 = 40 + 32 = 72.

The number outside the brackets multiplies every number inside. The area model is a picture of this.

Watch out for zeros: 206 is 200 + 6. There are no tens, so do not invent a 60.

Station 6 · Unit 3 Lessons 3–4

The standard algorithm and big numbers

How the algorithm works

1. Multiply the top number by the ones digit. Write that row.

2. Write a 0 in the ones place. Multiply by the tens digit. Write that row.

3. Add the two rows.

Example: 45 × 37

The two rows are the same as the area model's rows: 45 × 7 and 45 × 30.

Estimate to check

Round each number to its biggest place first: 3,567 × 24 ≈ 4,000 × 20 = 80,000.

The exact answer is 85,608, which is close. If your answer were 8,560 or 856,080, you would know something went wrong.

Station 7 · Unit 3 Lesson 5

Multiplication problems in the real world

Plan before you multiply

1. What is the question asking for?

2. Which numbers do I need? Some stories have extra numbers.

3. How many steps? Sometimes you add first, then multiply.

Two ways can both be right

Mona sold 402 kebabs, then 753 kebabs, at 83 g of meat each.

Amal: 402 × 83 + 753 × 83 = 95,865 g.

Sarah: (402 + 753) × 83 = 1,155 × 83 = 95,865 g.

Both are correct. This is the distributive property again.

Units matter

1 hour = 60 minutes · 1 week = 7 days · 1 m = 100 cm · 1 L = 1,000 mL

Write the unit with your answer.
