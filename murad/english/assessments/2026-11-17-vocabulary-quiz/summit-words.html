Summit Words

8 vocabulary words · Wonders Unit 2 Week 4 · She Had to Walk Before She Could Run · Nonfiction booklet pages 13–15

Step 1: Learn the 8 words

  • Book meaning is copied from your booklet, page 13.
  • In simple words says the same meaning in shorter words.
  • The black-and-white pictures and the sentences come from your booklet, page 14.
  • Press Read aloud to hear the word and its meaning.

Step 2: Your booklet, page 15

  • Part B has 8 sentences. Choose the word that fits the blank.
  • Part C has 8 sentences. Choose True or False.
  • If your answer is wrong, you get a hint and one more try.

Step 3: Practice quiz

  • 18 questions: choose the answer, or choose True or False.
  • One try for each question, like a real quiz. There is no timer.
  • At the end you see your score and the words to read again.
For parents: sources and answer notes
  • Words, book meanings, pictures and sentences: Gr. 6 Wonders Nonfiction Booklet 2026-2027 First Term, pages 13, 14 and 15 (Unit 2 Week 4 on the weekly outline; the booklet contents page labels it "Unit 4 Week 2"). Weekly outline Term 1 Week 8 lists "Vocabulary page 15" and "Vocabulary quiz".
  • Answer key: the school model answers in the repository cover the Nonfiction booklet only up to page 8, so no school key exists here for page 15. The answers on this page were worked out from the page 13 meanings. Part C sentence 6 ("held a summit in the backyard") is marked False because neither book meaning fits (not the top of a mountain, and not a meeting of officials from countries); please check it with the teacher if unsure.
  • Not from the book: the "In simple words" lines, the coloured line drawings, the example sentences for assess and implement (page 14 has no picture or sentence for those two), and all of Step 3. Step 3 is practice only, not the school quiz.
  • Design choices and their sources:
    • Muted colours, plain words, short sentences and bullets, descriptive buttons, one simple layout repeated: UK Home Office / GOV.UK "Designing for users on the autistic spectrum" poster (accessibility.blog.gov.uk).
    • Literal language, no idioms, little text per screen: W3C "Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities", pattern 4.4.4 Use Literal Language (w3.org/TR/coga-usable).
    • Pictures beside each word (visual supports), a hint after a wrong answer (prompting) and a star for each correct answer (reinforcement): three of the evidence-based practices in the NCAEP 2020 review, Hume et al. (NCAEP report; PMC8510990).
    • No moving animations, no sounds, no timer: WCAG 2.3.3 notes that motion can distract (w3.org).
